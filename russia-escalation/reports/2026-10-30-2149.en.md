## Rationale and qualifying criteria

1. **EU sabotage disclosure — 20/50 %.** October statements by [SRI](https://www.sri.ro/articole/comunicat-de-presa-26-10-2026.html) and [ABW](https://www.abw.gov.pl/pl/aktualnosci/2846,Podpalenie-stacji-Starlink-Od-ustalen-ABW-i-Policji-do-zatrzyman-w-Niemczech.html) maintain a nonzero expectation of another disclosure. A week provides limited room for a new case, a month more opportunities, but reporting may cluster. This is not a statistical frequency derived from two cases. What counts is the first joint fulfilment of material damage or an advanced physical attempt and explicit Russian attribution within the panel BfV, ABW, SRI, SGDSN, AIVD, BIS, Säpo, SUPO. **Alternative:** arrests weaken networks or the next announcement comes later; repeated coverage of a known case does not count.

2. **Czech disclosure — 2/6 %.** The narrow panel of [BIS](https://www.bis.cz/aktuality/), [NÚKIB](https://nukib.gov.cz/cs/infoservis/aktuality/) and the [MoD](https://mocr.mo.gov.cz/) publishes specific physical cases infrequently. No new case appeared in the available listings; this does not prove inactivity. The European context prevents zero, while a month allows delayed attribution. **Alternative:** a nonpublic case or an announcement by another authority fails this contract; an investigation already prepared for disclosure could instead produce a sudden announcement.

3. **Armed attack against NATO — 1/4 %.** The high threshold of deliberate weapon use by Russian state forces and physical effect, together with deterrence, outweighs the possibility of a local act. A month allows the crisis to change. [NATO](https://www.nato.int/en/what-we-do/deterrence-and-defence/strengthening-natos-eastern-flank) is historical context from 29 September here, not a fresh assessment of today's readiness. **Alternative:** a small deliberate incident could occur with limited publicly visible preparation. Two independent primary lines must establish the act; airspace violations, accidental spillover and civilian proxies alone are insufficient. An intention to “test NATO cohesion” does not have to be separately proved.

4. **Baltic armed attack — 0.5/2 %.** The same threshold, restricted to Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania; risk is below the broader NATO question. The [Latvian announcement](https://www.mod.gov.lv/en/news/eu-drone-and-counter-drone-technology-centre-be-developed-selija-military-training-area) develops capabilities in Latvia and the Netherlands, rather than confirming current Russian attack preparation. **Alternative:** a small border operation could have a weak public signature. Incomplete listings do not justify claiming that the current official threat assessment is unchanged.

5. **Public mobilisation order — 1/5 %.** A new binding federal order for at least 100,000 people has a higher political threshold than ongoing recruitment. [Today's contract-recruitment appeal](https://t.me/mod_russia/68644), read in the [MoD channel](https://t.me/s/mod_russia), neither establishes nor rules out such an order. A month allows a decision change under personnel pressure. **Alternative:** a decision may come suddenly or remain nonpublic; the legal portal and Kremlin were unavailable. Regular conscription, exercises and covert coercion do not automatically fulfil this narrow question.

6. **Belarusian entry — 0.5/2 %.** The previous supporting role and costs of direct entry give a low prior. Available listings from the [Belarusian](https://t.me/s/modmilby) and [Ukrainian MoDs](https://mod.gov.ua/en) do not provide a complete operational picture. What counts is a regular unit under Belarusian command in ground combat in Ukraine, established by two independent primary lines. **Alternative:** Moscow's pressure or a sudden incident changes the decision; absence of movements cannot be claimed. Russian units in Belarus, training and logistical support alone are insufficient.

7. **Nuclear demonstration/test — 0.2/1 %.** The prior for an actual weapon nuclear detonation outside a direct attack remains low; a month offers more room for a policy change. [IFE26](https://www.ctbto.org/IFE26) is a verification exercise. The known Bulava launch is not an established nuclear explosion. **Alternative:** a sudden decision to conduct an actual test. Some direct statements were unavailable and public [CTBTO listings](https://www.ctbto.org/resources/for-the-media/selected-statements-from-ctbto) are not live measurements. Yes requires two independent primary lines, including technical or competent state confirmation and established Russian responsibility.

8. **Nuclear weapon use — 0.1/0.5 %.** An attack with a weapon nuclear detonation against a military or populated target has an exceptionally high threshold; rhetoric and a missile launch do not establish it. I retain a nonzero prior for sudden catastrophic escalation, higher over a month. **Alternative:** a sharp military or political shock. Today's partial access cannot establish certainty about classified readiness; the same two independent primary lines are required as for a detonation outside an attack.

9. **Ukrainian emergency restrictions — 55/80 %.** The [primary Ukrenergo feed](https://t.me/s/Ukrenergo) shows continuing damage and scheduled restrictions. The [morning situation](https://t.me/Ukrenergo/5595) and [plan for 31 October](https://t.me/Ukrenergo/5596) do not themselves meet the strict threshold. Weekly vulnerability slightly outweighs repairs; a month allows further accumulation of damage, rather than independent daily trials. What counts is a new confirmation of actual emergency restrictions after Russian attacks in at least three explicitly named regions in one day. **Alternative:** repairs, imports and scheduled restrictions contain the situation, or the announcement does not name the required regions. Another operating day is a distinct case; a reprint for the same day is not.
